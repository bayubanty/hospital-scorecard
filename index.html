GEORGIA WATCH Hospital Performance Scorecard

METHODOLOGY

How we evaluate and score Georgia hospitals

Our Approach

The Georgia Hospital System Scorecard evaluates hospitals across four key dimensions of performance: Financial Transparency & Community Benefit, Community Benefit Spending, Healthcare Affordability & Billing, and Healthcare Access & Social Responsibility.

Our methodology combines publicly available data with proprietary analysis to provide a comprehensive view of hospital performance. Each hospital receives an overall grade (A-F) based on its composite score across all metrics.

Data Sources

We utilize data from multiple sources to ensure a comprehensive evaluation:

  • IRS Form 990 filings for nonprofit hospitals
  • Medicare Cost Reports
  • CMS Hospital Compare data
  • State health department reports
  • Hospital financial statements and annual reports
  • Community health needs assessments

Scoring System

Hospitals receive scores on a 0-100 scale for each metric, which are then converted to star ratings (1-5 stars). The overall grade is determined by weighting and combining these scores:

  • A (90-100): Excellent performance across all metrics
  • B (80-89): Strong performance with minor areas for improvement
  • C (70-79): Average performance with several areas needing attention
  • D (60-69): Below average performance with significant concerns
  • F (0-59): Poor performance requiring immediate improvement

Metric Definitions

Financial Transparency & Community Benefit

Balance Growth

Measures the hospital's financial growth and stability over time, including trends in net assets, operating margins, and reserves.

Transparency

Assessment of financial disclosure and reporting practices, including clarity of financial statements and accessibility of pricing information.

Fiscal Health

Overall financial stability and resource management, including debt levels, liquidity, and long-term sustainability.

Staffing

Adequacy and quality of healthcare staffing, including nurse-to-patient ratios, staff qualifications, and employee satisfaction.

Community Benefit Spending

Tax Benefit Utilization

Effective use of tax benefits for community programs, comparing tax exemption value to community benefit spending.

Quality of Community Benefit Spending

Effectiveness and impact of community benefit programs, including alignment with community health needs.

Strategic Use of Resources

Strategic allocation of community benefit resources to address identified community health priorities.

Healthcare Affordability & Billing

Financial Burden on Patients

Impact of healthcare costs on patients' financial wellbeing, including out-of-pocket expenses and medical debt.

Charity Care Provision

Extent and quality of charity care services provided to low-income and uninsured patients.

Medical Debt Management

Approaches to managing and preventing patient medical debt, including billing practices and financial assistance programs.

Healthcare Access & Social Responsibility

Range of Services

Diversity and comprehensiveness of healthcare services offered, including specialty care and preventive services.

Demographic Alignment

Alignment with community demographic needs, including services for vulnerable populations.

Workforce Training

Investment in staff training and development, including continuing education and career advancement opportunities.

Pay Equity Ratio

Fairness in compensation across the organization, including CEO-to-median-worker pay ratios.

Limitations

While we strive for comprehensive and accurate evaluations, our methodology has some limitations:

  • Data availability varies by hospital and may not reflect the most recent year
  • Some metrics rely on self-reported data from hospitals
  • Smaller hospitals may have less publicly available information
  • Our scoring system may not capture all nuances of hospital performance

We continuously work to improve our methodology and welcome feedback from hospitals and the public.
